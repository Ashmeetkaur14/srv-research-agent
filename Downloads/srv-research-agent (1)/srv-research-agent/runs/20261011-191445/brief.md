## 1. Snapshot
Sarvottam Cables Ltd is a manufacturer of power cables, wires, and conductors [S3]. In Q1 FY27, the company reported revenue from operations of ₹1,248 crore, an 18.0% increase from ₹1,058 crore in Q1 FY26 [S3]. Net profit rose 5.9% to ₹82 crore, while EBITDA margins contracted by 170 basis points to 11.2% compared to the year-ago period [S3].

## 2. Bull case
* **Strong Revenue and Order Growth:** Revenue grew 18.0% year-on-year, supported by an order book that reached ₹3,900 crore as of 30 June 2026, up from ₹3,150 crore a year prior [S3].
* **Capacity Expansion:** The company is on track to begin commercial production at its new Bharuch plant in Q3 FY27, which management says will add 30% to total cable capacity [S3].
* **Improving Shareholder Metrics:** Pledged promoter shares have significantly declined to 4.1% of promoter holding as of 30 June 2026, down from 11.5% in June 2025 [S8]. Additionally, institutional interest has grown, with FII and DII holdings increasing in the most recent quarter [S8].
* **Strategic Market Focus:** Management says data-centre cables currently represent 9% of domestic revenue and expects this segment to double within two years [S2].

## 3. Bear case
* **Margin Pressure:** EBITDA margins contracted to 11.2% in Q1 FY27 from 12.9% in Q1 FY26 [S3]. Management says this is due to a sharp rise in copper prices and expects to pass these costs to customers over the next two quarters [S3].
* **Working Capital Challenges:** Receivable days increased from 78 to 96 over the last year, which management says is driven by slower payments from two large state utility customers [S2].
* **Regulatory Exposure:** The company received a GST demand order of ₹46.3 crore for FY2020-21 and FY2021-22 [S7]. While management says they have a strong case to appeal and do not expect a material impact on operations, the financial liability remains outstanding [S7].
* **Contractual Lag:** Approximately two-thirds of contracts include price-variation clauses, but these reset with a lag of one to two quarters, leaving the company exposed to raw material price volatility in the interim [S2].
